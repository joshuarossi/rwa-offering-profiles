Overview

HELX turns the long-dated, contracted cash flows of an operating solar plant into a quarterly-paying token. No construction risk, no merchant power exposure for 15 years.

Helios Solar Yield (HELX) is a series of revenue-participation notes issued by Helios Renewable Finance Ltd. and secured by a first-ranking charge over the shares and receivables of Helios Park I S.L., the owner of a 48.2 MWp solar park near Écija, Seville province, one of the sunniest locations in mainland Europe.

The plant reached commercial operation in March 2025 and sells 100% of its output to Iberdrola under a 15-year power purchase agreement at €52.40/MWh, indexed to Spanish CPI. Operations and maintenance are contracted to the EPC contractor under a 10-year full-wrap agreement with an availability guarantee of 98.5%.

Note holders receive 85% of distributable cash flow, paid quarterly in EUR or USDC, with the balance retained by the sponsor as a subordinated interest. Notes amortise from year 6 and mature in 2041, matching the PPA term.

Security & Cash Waterfall

All PPA revenue is paid into a project account controlled by the security trustee, Intertrust (Spain) S.L. Each quarter, cash is applied in order to: operating costs and taxes, O&M and insurance, the debt service reserve top-up, and then 85% to HELX holders and 15% to the sponsor.

HELX holders benefit from a first-ranking pledge over the shares of Helios Park I S.L., its bank accounts and its receivables under the PPA. In an enforcement, the trustee may sell the project company and apply proceeds to the notes.

Risk Factors

An investment in HELX involves a high degree of risk. Prospective investors should carefully consider the following, together with the full risk disclosure in the offering memorandum.

Principal Risks

  1. Resource and generation risk. Solar irradiance varies year to year. Sustained underperformance versus the P50 forecast would reduce distributions.
  2. Offtaker credit risk. Revenue depends on Iberdrola honouring the PPA for 15 years. A downgrade or default would expose the plant to merchant power prices.
  3. Operational and equipment risk. Inverter failures, module degradation above forecast, grid outages, or extreme weather such as hail could reduce output. Insurance and warranties may not fully compensate.
  4. Regulatory and political risk. Changes to Spanish or EU energy regulation, taxation of generation, or grid access rules could adversely affect revenue.
  5. Structural subordination and enforcement risk. Enforcement of Spanish security can take time. In a distressed sale, proceeds may be insufficient to repay notes in full.
  6. Liquidity risk. The notes are long-dated. There is no assurance of an active secondary market on the platform or elsewhere.
  7. Currency risk. Distributions are denominated in EUR. Investors in other currencies bear exchange-rate movements.
